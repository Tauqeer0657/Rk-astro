## What is Benign Prostate Enlargement (BPH)?

Benign Prostate Enlargement, also called BPH, is a common condition in older men. The prostate is a small gland below the bladder. As men age, this gland can grow larger. Although this growth is not cancer, it can cause problems. For example, an enlarged prostate can press on the urethra, which is the tube that carries urine out of the body. As a result, men may notice changes in how they urinate. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), BPH affects many men over 50. However, not all men with BPH have symptoms.

## Common Symptoms of BPH

Many men with BPH notice changes in their urination. These are called enlarged prostate symptoms. Early signs may be mild, but they can get worse over time. For instance, you may notice:

-   Needing to urinate more often, especially at night
-   Feeling a sudden urge to urinate
-   Having trouble starting to urinate
-   Weak or slow urine stream
-   Stopping and starting while urinating
-   Feeling that your bladder is not empty after urinating

Sometimes, BPH can cause urine leaks or make it hard to fully empty the bladder. If you notice these symptoms, it is important to talk to a doctor. Early treatment can help prevent problems.

## Causes and Risk Factors

BPH is not caused by cancer. Instead, it happens as men get older. The exact cause is not clear. However, several factors can increase your risk. For example:

-   Age: BPH is rare before age 40, but common after 50
-   Family history: If your father or brother had BPH, your risk is higher
-   Lifestyle: Lack of exercise and obesity may raise your risk
-   Other health problems: Diabetes and heart disease can also play a role

Although these factors increase risk, not all men with them will get BPH. Still, knowing your risk can help you watch for symptoms.

## Diagnosis of BPH

If you have symptoms of BPH, your doctor will ask about your health and do a physical exam. Often, they will check your prostate by feeling it through the rectum. In addition, your doctor may order tests, such as:

-   Urine tests to check for infection or other problems
-   Blood tests to check kidney health
-   Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) test to rule out prostate cancer
-   Ultrasound to see the size of your prostate

These tests help your doctor find out if BPH is causing your symptoms. Sometimes, other conditions can cause similar problems, so proper diagnosis is important.

## Treatment Options for BPH

There are many BPH treatment options. The best choice depends on your symptoms and health. For mild symptoms, your doctor may suggest watchful waiting. This means you check your symptoms regularly but do not start treatment right away. If symptoms bother you, treatment may include:

-   **Medicines:** Some drugs relax the muscles in the prostate and bladder. Others shrink the prostate over time.
-   **Minimally invasive procedures:** These use heat, lasers, or other methods to remove part of the prostate.
-   **Surgery:** In severe cases, surgery may be needed to remove prostate tissue.

Each treatment has risks and benefits. Therefore, talk with your doctor to find the best option for you. According to the CDC, most men find relief with proper care.

## Lifestyle Tips and Prevention

While you cannot always prevent BPH, some habits may help manage benign prostate enlargement. For example:

-   Stay active with regular exercise
-   Eat a healthy diet rich in fruits and vegetables
-   Limit caffeine and alcohol, which can irritate the bladder
-   Try to urinate regularly and do not rush
-   Maintain a healthy weight

In addition, regular check-ups can help catch problems early. If you notice changes in your urination, see your doctor. Early action can make a big difference.

In summary, benign prostate enlargement is common and treatable, but its symptoms can significantly affect your daily life if left unaddressed.
